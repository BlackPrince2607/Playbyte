1. Executive Summary
Playbyte turns the passive scroll into a live, participatory feed. Every screen is a short, self-contained "play" — a prediction, poll, or reaction to what's happening right now — that takes under five seconds to join. The product is positioned participation-first: the reward is seeing yourself inside a real-time crowd, not climbing a leaderboard or beating a friend.
This document defines the MVP required to validate the core participation loop, plus the two follow-on phases (V1.1 Fast Follow and V2 Growth) that build out social depth, content variety, and monetization once the loop is proven.
2. Problem Statement & Opportunity
2.1 Problem
Passive content feeds (video, articles, images) ask nothing of the user beyond attention — engagement is shallow and interchangeable across apps.
Existing trivia/quiz and prediction apps lean heavily on competition and skill display, which excludes casual users who don't want to be ranked or judged.
Real-time cultural moments (breaking news, live sports, viral debates) currently have no low-friction, purpose-built way for a casual user to "weigh in" and see how they compare to everyone else, instantly.
2.2 Opportunity
A feed where the unit of content is a 5-second participatory "play" instead of a video or post, combining the format habits of short-video apps with the instant-gratification loop of live polling.
Positioning around participation rather than competition broadens the addressable audience beyond gamers/quiz enthusiasts to anyone with an opinion.
Live crowd counters and topical content create natural, non-paid virality ("everyone's talking about this right now").
3. Product Vision & Positioning
Vision:  Every swipe is a play — and every play connects you to everyone else playing it, right now.
Positioning statement:  For people who want a quick, social, no-pressure way to react to what's happening, Playbyte is a live participation feed where every card shows you how you fit into the crowd — unlike trivia and prediction apps that reward skill and rank, Playbyte rewards simply showing up.

7. MVP Feature Requirements
MVP scope is limited to what is required to prove that users will repeatedly join live, participatory moments and feel a sense of belonging from doing so. Priority key: P0 = launch-blocking, P1 = strongly desired, P2 = nice-to-have if time allows.
7.1 Live Moment Feed (core)
7.1.1 Swipeable feed of moments [P0] 
The primary surface of the app: a full-screen, vertically swipeable feed where each card is one participatory moment.
User story:  As a user, I want to swipe through short, self-contained moments so that I can participate in many of them quickly without deciding what to do next.
Functional requirements:
Feed renders one moment card at a time, full-viewport, TikTok-style vertical swipe navigation
Supports three MVP card types: Predict (binary yes/no), Pulse (2–4 option poll), Reaction (live binary reaction to an in-progress event)
Cards preload the next 3 in the stack to avoid load delay on swipe
Each card shows: category tag, prompt text, response controls, live crowd counter, friend-presence avatars
User response is captured on tap/swipe of an answer option, not on card exit, so partial engagement is never silently lost
After responding, the card updates in place to show the live result (crowd meter or ticker) for 1–2 seconds before auto-advancing or allowing manual swipe
Feed ordering blends chronological "live now" moments with a lightly personalized ranking based on the user's selected interest categories
Acceptance criteria:
A user can respond to a moment and see an updated live result within 2 seconds on a standard 4G connection
Swiping up always advances to a new, previously unseen moment (no immediate repeats within a session)
Feed never shows an empty or loading state for more than 500ms under normal network conditions
Feature success metric:  Median moments joined per session ≥ 6
7.1.2 Live crowd counter & aggregation engine [P0] 
Real-time backend service that aggregates responses per moment and streams updated counts to active viewers.
User story:  As a user, I want to see how many people are playing a moment with me right now so that participating feels alive, not static.
Functional requirements:
Each moment tracks total responses and per-option breakdown, updated in near-real-time
Client receives live count updates via a persistent connection (WebSocket or equivalent), with polling fallback
Counter displays using abbreviated formatting for large numbers (e.g., 48.2K)
A rolling "joined in the last minute" ticker is available for Pulse and Predict cards
System distinguishes and correctly labels low-volume moments (e.g., "be the first 50 to weigh in") rather than displaying a misleading small number as if it were a mature crowd
Acceptance criteria:
Counter updates reach 95% of active viewers within 2 seconds of a new response
No moment ever displays a fabricated or non-live number; all displayed counts are traceable to real recorded responses
Feature success metric:  ≥ 99% of P0 moments reach at least 500 responses within 10 minutes of going live
7.1.3 Friend presence [P1] 
Shows which of a user's connected friends have already engaged with a given moment.
User story:  As a user, I want to see that my friends already played this moment so that the app feels social even when I'm playing alone.
Functional requirements:
Friend graph built from contact sync (opt-in) and in-app follow/add
Card displays a small avatar stack of friends who responded, with a count of additional friends
Tapping the avatar stack shows friends' responses (respecting each friend's individual privacy setting)
Users can set response visibility to Friends-only, Public, or Private per account-level default (overridable per moment)
Acceptance criteria:
Friend avatars appear on a card only after contact sync or in-app connection consent is explicitly granted
A user with response visibility set to Private never appears in another user's friend-presence stack
Feature success metric:  ≥ 30% of MAU have at least 3 mutual connections by week 4
7.2 Onboarding & Personalization
7.2.1 Lightweight interest selection [P0] 
A fast, low-friction onboarding flow that captures topical interests without gating access behind a skill quiz.
User story:  As a new user, I want to start playing within seconds of opening the app, without answering a battery of setup questions.
Functional requirements:
Onboarding presents a single screen of 8–12 category chips (Sports, News, Pop Culture, Food, Weather, Tech, etc.); user selects any number, including none
Skipping interest selection is always allowed and defaults to a general/trending mix
First moment is served within 10 seconds of app open on first launch
Optional (skippable) contact sync and notification permission prompts follow, not precede, the first played moment
Acceptance criteria:
A new user can reach and respond to their first moment in 3 taps or fewer from app install
No mandatory account creation gate blocks first-moment participation (guest play allowed, with soft prompt to save progress after 3 moments)
Feature success metric:  ≥ 85% of installs reach their first played moment
7.3 Notifications
7.3.1 Moment alerts [P1] 
Push notifications that surface time-sensitive, live moments to re-engage users at the right cultural moment.
User story:  As a user, I want to be notified when something worth reacting to is happening live so I don't miss the moment while it's hot.
Functional requirements:
Notification types: Live Now (event-tied, e.g., live game reaction), Trending Pulse (a poll gaining rapid response volume), Friend Activity (a friend just joined a moment you haven't seen)
Default notification frequency capped at 3 per day; user-configurable in settings
Tapping a notification deep-links directly into that specific moment card
Acceptance criteria:
Users can fully disable each notification type independently
No notification is sent for a moment that has already expired or closed
Feature success metric:  Notification-driven session share ≥ 20% of DAU sessions
7.4 Recap & Participation Stats
7.4.1 Weekly participation recap [P1] 
A non-competitive summary of the user's participation, framed around belonging rather than skill or rank.
User story:  As a user, I want to see a summary of how much I showed up this week so that I feel recognized without being judged against others.
Functional requirements:
Recap surfaces: moments joined, % of moments where the user's answer matched the majority, consecutive days active, total unique people played alongside
No numeric skill score, accuracy leaderboard position, or public rank is shown at MVP
Participation badges awarded for engagement patterns (e.g., topic regular), not for being "right" more than others
Recap is shareable as an image card
Acceptance criteria:
Recap screen contains zero head-to-head comparison against any named individual friend
Recap generates correctly for a user with as few as 1 moment played in the period (no divide-by-zero or broken states)
Feature success metric:  ≥ 40% of WAU view their weekly recap
7.5 Profile
7.5.1 Basic profile [P0] 
Minimal identity surface required to support friend presence and recap features.
User story:  As a user, I want a simple profile so friends can recognize me in the app.
Functional requirements:
Fields: display name, avatar (preset or uploaded photo), optional short bio
Privacy controls for response visibility (see 7.1.3) live on the profile screen
No visible follower/following counts or public leaderboard rank at MVP
Acceptance criteria:
A user can complete profile setup in under 30 seconds
7.6 Sharing
7.6.1 Share a moment result [P1] 
Lets a user export a visual card of their response and the live crowd result to share outside the app.
User story:  As a user, I want to share how I answered a moment (and how the crowd answered) so I can bring friends into the app.
Functional requirements:
Generates a branded image card with the moment prompt, the user's answer, and the crowd result
Native OS share sheet integration (Messages, Instagram Stories, etc.)
Shared link deep-links a recipient directly into the live (or closed-result) version of that moment
Acceptance criteria:
Share flow completes in 2 taps from the result screen
Feature success metric:  Share-to-install conversion ≥ 12%
7.7 Content Operations (internal tooling)
7.7.1 Moment CMS & scheduling [P0] 
Internal tool for the editorial/content team to create, schedule, and retire moments — this is launch-blocking infrastructure, not a user-facing feature.
User story:  As a content editor, I want to create and schedule moments in advance and in real time so the feed always has fresh, relevant content.
Functional requirements:
Form-based creation of Predict, Pulse, and Reaction card types with category tagging
Scheduling for future release and manual "go live now" trigger for breaking moments
Restricted-topics guardrail: card types tied to health, tragedy, or election outcomes require a second editorial approval before publishing
Basic moderation queue for reported content or flagged trending moments
Acceptance criteria:
An editor can publish a live moment to the production feed in under 2 minutes
No moment tagged as a restricted topic can go live without a second approver on record

8. Post-MVP Feature Requirements
8.1 V1.1 — Fast Follow (Month 2–4)
Focus: deepen the social layer and expand content variety once the core participation loop is validated.
8.1.1 Comments & reactions on a moment [P1] 
Lightweight commentary layer so users can react to a moment's live result, not just the prompt.
Functional requirements:
Emoji-first quick reactions on a moment result screen
Threaded text comments, moderated via the same content-ops tooling as moment creation
8.1.2 Groups / private moments [P1] 
Lets a user create a private moment visible only to an invited friend group (e.g., "just us watching the game").
Functional requirements:
User-initiated moment creation restricted to closed friend groups
Group-scoped crowd counter and friend-presence view
8.1.3 Opt-in duels [P2] 
Reintroduces 1:1 challenge mechanics as an optional mode, not the default feed experience — preserves participation-first positioning for the core feed.
Functional requirements:
Duels live in a separate, opt-in section of the app, not injected into the main live feed
No duel result affects the main profile's participation stats
8.1.4 Sponsored moments [P1] 
First monetization surface: brand-sponsored Pulse or Predict cards inserted into the feed with clear disclosure.
Functional requirements:
Sponsored cards visually labeled and disclosed per applicable ad-disclosure regulations
Sponsored card frequency capped (e.g., no more than 1 in every 12 organic cards)
Brand dashboard (internal, sales-facing) showing live participation volume and result breakdown for a sponsored moment
8.1.5 Content format expansion [P2] 
Adds lightweight puzzle and quiz formats back in, framed as participation ("how did everyone else do") rather than individual scoring.
Functional requirements:
New card type: daily puzzle with a crowd-average completion time shown instead of individual leaderboard rank
8.2 V2 — Growth (Month 5+)
Focus: scale the participation loop through creator tools, richer live formats, and monetization maturity.
8.2.1 Creator tools [P1] 
Allows a subset of trusted users (and eventually all users) to submit moments for editorial review and publishing.
Functional requirements:
Submission flow reusing the internal CMS's restricted-topic guardrails
Creator attribution on published user-generated moments
8.2.2 Live audio/video-tied moments [P2] 
Ties moments to live audio/video streams (e.g., reacting in real time alongside a live broadcast) for major cultural events.
Functional requirements:
Sync layer between a live broadcast timestamp and moment go-live triggers
8.2.3 Localization & international expansion [P1] 
Expands content operations and the app itself to additional languages and regional content calendars.
Functional requirements:
Locale-aware content scheduling
Full UI localization starting with the top 3 target markets
8.2.4 Brand analytics dashboard [P1] 
Self-serve analytics for sponsors to view participation, sentiment split, and reach of their sponsored moments.
Functional requirements:
Exportable reporting
Real-time dashboard during a live sponsored moment
8.2.5 Pro tier [P2] 
Optional paid tier offering deeper personal stats, an ad-free feed, and early access to trending moments.
Functional requirements:
Subscription billing integration
Feature-gating layer for Pro-only surfaces

9. Non-Functional Requirements
9.1 Performance
Card-to-card swipe transition renders in under 100ms on supported devices
Live counter update latency: 95th percentile under 2 seconds
Cold app start to first interactive moment: under 3 seconds on a mid-tier device
9.2 Scalability & Reliability
Real-time aggregation infrastructure must support at least 100,000 concurrent connected sessions at MVP launch scale, with a clear scaling path to 1M+
Target platform availability: 99.9% monthly uptime
Graceful degradation: if the live-count stream drops, the client falls back to periodic polling rather than showing a broken counter
9.3 Privacy & Data
Contact sync and friend-graph features are strictly opt-in with clear, revocable consent
Compliance with applicable regional data regulations (e.g., GDPR, CCPA), including data export and deletion requests
Minimum age gate consistent with applicable regulations; no behavioral advertising targeting to users below the regulated age threshold
Response-level privacy settings (Public / Friends-only / Private) are enforced server-side, not just in the client UI
9.4 Accessibility
WCAG 2.1 AA conformance for all core screens
All swipe-based interactions have an equivalent tap/button alternative for users who cannot perform swipe gestures
Screen-reader labeling for live counters, avatar stacks, and card categories
9.5 Trust & Safety
Restricted-topics editorial guardrail (Section 7.7.1) is enforced at the CMS level, not left to individual editor judgment alone
User reporting available on every moment and every comment (post-V1.1)
No prediction moment may use monetary stakes, odds, or payout language, to avoid gambling-adjacent framing
10. Out of Scope (MVP)
Real-money wagering or gambling mechanics of any kind
Skill-based matchmaking, ranked ladders, or public leaderboards at MVP
User-generated moment creation (creator tools) — deferred to V2
Web app / desktop experience — mobile native only at MVP
Paid subscription tier — deferred until V1.1 monetization pilot
11. Risks & Mitigations
Risk
Impact
Mitigation
Cold-start crowd size
Live counters feel hollow without real concurrent volume
Seed launch in a small number of time zones/markets; use editorial team to guarantee moment cadence; simulate honestly-labeled aggregate context where volume is genuinely low
Sensitive-topic predictions
Predictions on politics, tragedy, or health events can create reputational or moderation risk
Maintain a restricted-topics list; require editorial sign-off on all live moments at MVP (no open user submission)
Real-time infrastructure cost
Sub-2-second crowd counter updates at scale require persistent connections and fan-out
Start with a managed pub/sub (e.g., WebSocket gateway + Redis) sized for MVP concurrency; revisit before V2 scale
Perceived gambling adjacency
"Predict" cards without stakes can still read as betting to some users or regulators
No odds/payout language, no monetary framing; legal review of prediction copy before launch
Content ops throughput
Live, topical content requires a fast editorial pipeline; understaffing stalls the feed
Build the CMS and scheduling tool as an MVP-blocking workstream, not an afterthought
12. Open Questions
Do we launch in a single metro/timezone first to guarantee crowd density, or go broad and thin?
What is the minimum viable editorial team size to sustain 15–20 fresh moments/day?
How do we badge or gate topics that are borderline (e.g., close sports games, election-adjacent news) without over-restricting relevance?
Is push notification cadence for "live" moments opt-in only, or default-on with a frequency cap?
